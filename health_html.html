How to Perform Self-Health Measurements in Space

Not every crew member is a flight surgeon. Follow these standardized NASA microgravity and partial-gravity procedures to capture accurate telemetry without Earth-based lab equipment:

1. Heart Rate & Blood Pressure (Arm Cuff / Photoplethysmography)

Rest seated or anchored in foot restraints for 5 minutes. Keep the brachial cuff at heart level (in microgravity, hydrostatic pressure gradients disappear, so arm position relative to the right atrium is critical). Trigger two readings 60 seconds apart and log the average.

2. Blood Glucose (Capillary Fingerstick Glucometer)

Sanitize fingertip with alcohol wipe and allow to dry completely. In microgravity, surface tension keeps the blood droplet spherical on the finger—touch the test strip edge directly to the bead before moving your hand. Normal fasting range: 70–100 mg/dL (post-meal <140 mg/dL).

3. Core Temperature & Ocular SANS Check

Use the temporal artery scanner across the forehead or tympanic ear probe. For Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS), use the handheld tonometer after topical anesthetic drops and complete the Amsler grid visual distortion test.

4. Radiation Dosimeter & Behavioral/Sleep Log

Sync your wearable Crew Active Dosimeter (CAD) via Bluetooth/USB to record cumulative millisieverts (mSv). Log sleep duration from actigraphy wrist sensors and rate cognitive alertness honestly prior to EVA or robotic operations.
